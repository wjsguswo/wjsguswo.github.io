Robots and vehicles that keep working when conditions stop cooperating.

I am a postdoctoral researcher at DGIST in Daegu, Korea. I work on vision-language-action models for manipulation and on bringing them to humanoid robots.

My Ph.D. was about autonomous driving in heavy rain: measuring how far perception degrades when the camera is blocked, generating realistic rainy data to train on, and restoring images so the vehicle can keep driving. What connects that work to robot learning is one question, which is what a learned system does once the world departs from its training data.

Research

Robust vision-language-action policies

2026 to now, DGIST Physical AI research group

Policies trained on demonstrations tend to fail when the robot or the scene is somewhere the demonstrations never visited. I study how to make vision-language-action policies hold up in those states, test them on perturbation benchmarks in simulation, and work toward running them on humanoid and dual-arm platforms.

Autonomous driving in adverse weather

2021 to 2026, Ph.D. at DGIST

Rain on a camera lens hides lane markings and produces false detections. I built a simulator-based framework that measures how lane detection degrades as rainfall increases, a style-diversification module that synthesizes varied rain for training multi-task driving perception, and restoration methods that recover the image before perception runs.

Image generation and field robots

Collaborations

Brightness-controllable image generation for night driving scenes, and a lane-tracking control system for a robot that repaints road lane markings.
