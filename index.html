About

I am a postdoctoral researcher at DGIST in Daegu, Korea, where I have studied and worked since my undergraduate years. My current work is on vision-language-action (VLA) models: how to post-train them so they stay reliable outside their demonstrations, and how to bring them onto humanoid robots.

I received my Ph.D. from DGIST in 2026. My dissertation asked how degraded visual observations change what an autonomous vehicle perceives and how it drives, and it developed three answers: a way to measure the degradation, data generation that reproduces it for training, and restoration that removes it before perception runs. The question I carry into robot learning is the same one. What does a learned system do once the world departs from its training data?

Research

Robust vision-language-action policies

2026 to now, DGIST Physical AI Research Center

Policies trained on demonstrations tend to fail when the robot or the scene is somewhere the demonstrations never visited. I study how to make VLA policies hold up in those states, test them on perturbation benchmarks in simulation, and work toward running them on humanoid and dual-arm platforms.

Autonomous driving in adverse weather

2021 to 2026, Ph.D. at DGIST

Rain on a camera lens hides lane markings and produces false detections. I built a simulator-based framework that measures how lane detection degrades as rainfall increases, a style-diversification module that synthesizes varied rain for training multi-task driving perception, and a severity-aware diffusion model that restores images under several kinds of degradation.

Night scenes, stereo and field robots

Collaborations at DGIST

Brightness-controllable image generation and night-to-day translation for driving data, domain-adaptive stereo matching, and a bird's-eye-view lane-tracking controller for a robot that repaints road lane markings.
